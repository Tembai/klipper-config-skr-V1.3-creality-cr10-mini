## Symptom

Added a `[screws_tilt_adjust]` section (2026-08-13) to get quick 4-corner screw
turn advice via `SCREWS_TILT_CALCULATE`. Running `G28` then
`SCREWS_TILT_CALCULATE` remotely crashed the nozzle straight into the glass bed,
with the BLTouch pin ending up hanging off the edge of the bed at the same
physical spot every time. This happened 3 separate times across two different
sessions (once with the original screw coordinates, once after a first
attempted fix, and once more after asking the user to power-cycle the printer).
The user had to kill power to the printer twice to stop the crash.

## First (WRONG) hypothesis: screw coordinates exceed the safe/reachable area

The original config copied `[bed_screws]`'s coordinates directly:
`screw1: 30,30 / screw2: 270,30 / screw3: 30,210 / screw4: 270,210`. Since
`x_offset: -37.0` and the probe needs `nozzle_x = target_x + 37` to land on a
given target (so reasoned at the time), `screw2`/`screw3`/`screw4` at X=270
would need nozzle X=307 - past `stepper_x`'s `position_max: 300` and off the
physical glass. Coordinates were tightened to stay within the already-proven-
safe `[bed_mesh]` bounds (`mesh_min: 20,20` / `mesh_max: 250,205`): `screw2:
240,30 / screw3: 240,195 / screw4: 30,195`. Deployed and verified live via
klippy.log.

**This did not fix it.** The crash recurred identically, including at
`screw1: 30,30` - a corner well inside the "safe" bounds and a coordinate that
was never touched by the fix above. This should have been the tell that the
reachability theory was wrong.

## Second (ALSO WRONG) hypothesis: BLTouch hardware fault

After the coordinate fix didn't help, and the crash reproduced identically
(same "no trigger" style failure) on a clean retry after a full printer
power-cycle, it was wrongly concluded that the BLTouch had a genuine,
reproducible hardware/firmware fault causing it to fail to trigger. Both
`[screws_tilt_adjust]` and `[gcode_macro BED_MESH_CALIBRATE_FULL]` were
disabled and `stepper_z`'s `position_min` was tightened from -5 to -1.5mm as a
crash-depth backstop, pending "physical hardware diagnosis."

**This was also wrong.** The user pointed out the probe triggers correctly
when pressed by hand, and that the probe hung off the bed at the exact same
place every single time - which is a strong signal of a deterministic
coordinate bug, not a random/intermittent hardware fault.

## Actual root cause: screws_tilt_adjust needs NOZZLE coordinates, not probe-target coordinates

Per Klipper's own docs (Config_Reference.html#screws_tilt_adjust): "screw1: X, Y
- ...This is a position to command the **nozzle** to, so that the **probe** is
directly above the bed screw." Unlike `[bed_mesh]` (whose `mesh_min`/`mesh_max`
ARE probe-relative and auto-adjusted internally by Klipper), `screws_tilt_adjust`
does **not** auto-apply the probe's `x_offset`/`y_offset` - the config author
must already have computed the nozzle position that puts the probe over the
target.

The bug: `[bed_screws]`'s coordinates (30,30 / 270,30 / 30,210 / 270,210) are
correctly meant as direct nozzle-over-screw positions for that module (no probe
involved in a manual paper test). These were copied straight into
`[screws_tilt_adjust]` with no offset adjustment at all. With `x_offset: -37.0`,
`y_offset: -7.0`, commanding the nozzle to (30,30) puts the probe at
`(30-37, 30-7) = (-7, 23)` - off the bed to the left, at X=-7. Since screw1 was
never changed across "fixes," this same wrong probe position (-7,23) was
computed identically every single attempt, exactly matching the user's
observation of the probe hanging off the bed at the same spot every time. Z then
kept lowering with no floor to trigger against until the nozzle hit the bed.

## Fix (2026-08-15)

Re-derived `screw1..4` as NOZZLE coordinates = desired probe target + offset:
- Desired probe targets (same corners already proven safe via bed_mesh):
  (30,30), (250,30), (250,205), (30,205)
- Nozzle offset for this probe: +37 X, +7 Y
- Resulting config: `screw1: 67,37 / screw2: 287,37 / screw3: 287,212 /
  screw4: 67,212` - all within `stepper_x`/`stepper_y` position_max (300/220).

`[screws_tilt_adjust]` and `BED_MESH_CALIBRATE_FULL` were both re-enabled
(bed_mesh's mesh_min/mesh_max were never buggy - only screws_tilt_adjust's
raw/unconverted coordinates were). `stepper_z position_min` was kept at -1.5mm
(tightened from -5) as cheap defense-in-depth, independent of this fix.

## Lesson for next time

- Different Klipper leveling/calibration modules have DIFFERENT coordinate
  conventions: `[bed_mesh]` mesh_min/mesh_max and `[axis_twist_compensation]`
  calibrate_* are probe-relative (Klipper auto-adjusts). `[bed_screws]`,
  `[screws_tilt_adjust]`, `[bed_tilt]` points, `[z_tilt]` points, and
  `[quad_gantry_level]` points are all **nozzle** coordinates - the offset must
  be applied manually by whoever writes the config. Never assume one module's
  coordinate convention carries over to another; check the docs for each.
- A crash that recurs at the EXACT SAME physical spot every time, unaffected by
  supposedly-fixing changes to other coordinates, is a strong signal of a
  deterministic math/config bug - not an intermittent hardware fault. Don't
  jump to "hardware fault" as a conclusion without first re-deriving the
  relevant coordinate math from the actual Klipper docs.
- NEVER remotely retry a probe-based command (`G28`, `SCREWS_TILT_CALCULATE`,
  `BED_MESH_CALIBRATE`, `PROBE_ACCURACY`) more than once after it fails/crashes
  without stopping to properly root-cause it. Two "fixes" were deployed and
  retried unsupervised before the real bug was found, causing the same crash a
  total of 3 times and requiring the user to kill power twice.
